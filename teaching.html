Teaching Practice Experiences

From Observation to Application

Teaching Plan

1

Curriculum

teaching-1

One challenge I encountered during my teaching practice was adapting to a different curriculum. At Yayasan Sekolah Kristen Indonesia (YSKI), the school follows the Kurikulum Merdeka, while Smart Classes also incorporate the Cambridge approach. I was assigned to teach Grade 7 students and handle lessons on the Present Simple Tense and Present Continuous Tense.

What made the experience more challenging was understanding a lesson plan format that was different from what we use in the Philippines. Their lesson planning integrates reflection activities, Christian values, and the Pancasila Student Profile. Through this experience, I learned how to make my lessons more holistic by combining language learning with values formation and student-centred learning.

2

Teaching Plan Related to My Major

teaching-2

Before my teaching demonstration, I spent time preparing lesson plans that aligned with the learning competencies assigned by my mentor teacher. Since the lesson plan format was different from what we use in the Philippines, I had to adapt to a more holistic structure that integrated reflection activities, Christian values, and the Pancasila Student Profile.

I also reviewed the Gold Experience textbook by Pearson and identified the topics I would teach in Grade 7. This preparation helped me understand the learners' needs and allowed me to organise activities appropriate for the classroom setting.

Teaching Practice

1

Classroom Demonstrations

My teaching demonstrations were conducted on 14–15 September 2026 with the Grade 7 sections of YSKI. These two days became one of the most valuable learning experiences throughout my SEA-Teacher journey.

I taught lessons on the Present Simple Tense and Present Continuous Tense using an engaging, explorer-themed approach. To create an engaging learning environment, I introduced the classroom cue, "Explorers!" with the response "On the Quest!", which helped capture students' attention and establish classroom interaction.

Throughout the lessons, students participated in a variety of activities, including the Signage Challenge, Draw and Guess Challenge, group tasks, and online games. These activities encouraged learners to apply grammar concepts in meaningful ways while working collaboratively with their classmates. Students were given opportunities to share ideas, answer questions, and demonstrate their understanding through both individual and group discussions.

Teaching different Grade 7 sections allowed me to observe the unique personalities and learning styles of each class. Some classes were more reserved, while others were highly participative and energetic. These experiences helped me understand the importance of adapting instructional strategies according to students' needs and classroom dynamics.

One of my favourite moments was seeing students actively engage in the activities, volunteer answers, and help one another during discussions. Watching learners become more involved in the lesson made the teaching experience both meaningful and rewarding.

2

Challenges and Adaptations

Several challenges emerged during my teaching demonstrations. Some students were hesitant to participate, while others required additional support to understand the lesson concepts. Differences in class profiles also demanded different teaching approaches. In addition, unstable internet connectivity affected some planned online activities.

To address these challenges, I revised portions of my lesson plan, modified instructions, adjusted classroom activities, and introduced reward badges to encourage participation. These adaptations helped improve student engagement and allowed me to create a more interactive learning environment.

3

Teaching Highlights

One of the most rewarding aspects of my teaching practice was witnessing students become more confident and actively involved in classroom activities. Watching them volunteer answers, collaborate with their classmates, and participate enthusiastically reminded me why I chose the teaching profession.

The experience taught me that effective teaching is not about having a perfect lesson plan, but about understanding learners' needs, adapting when necessary, and creating meaningful opportunities for learning.

4

Cultural Presentation

After completing my teaching demonstrations, I was given the opportunity to introduce the Philippines to the students through a cultural presentation. Wearing a Filipiniana, I shared information about Filipino culture, traditions, festivals, language, tourist destinations, and everyday life.

What I expected to be a simple presentation became one of the most memorable experiences of my stay in Indonesia. Students from different sections warmly welcomed me and showed genuine curiosity about the Philippines. They eagerly participated in activities, practised Tagalog expressions, and asked thoughtful questions about Filipino culture and life in the Philippines.

One unforgettable moment happened during my presentation in Grade 8B when two students surprised me with a portrait sketch of me wearing a Filipiniana. It was my first time receiving such a gift from students, and I was deeply touched by their thoughtfulness.

More importantly, the activity allowed me to build stronger connections with the learners. Through their kindness, questions, drawings, compliments, and heartfelt interactions, I experienced the true value of cultural exchange. I even witnessed some students expressing sadness when they learned that my stay at YSKI would soon come to an end.

That moment reminded me that teaching is not only about sharing knowledge but also about building relationships that leave a lasting impact on both teachers and learners. This experience became one of the reasons why Indonesia eventually became the place where I discovered that my passion truly meets purpose.
